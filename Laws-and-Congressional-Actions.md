## 1. Key Congressional Measures & Statutory Restrictions

<details open>
<summary><strong>Click to Expand: Legislative Track Record on Saudi Assistance & Yemen/Houthi Intervention</strong></summary>
<br>

- **Foreign Operations Appropriations (Pub. L. 109-102, 2005):** Early statutory drafting limiting or barring direct funding to Saudi Arabia.
- **Department of Defense Appropriations Bill (S. 2474, 116th Cong., 2019):** Legislative pushback against unauthorized intervention in the conflict involving the Houthis.
- **Khanna Amendment (House action, September 2021):** Effort to end U.S. logistical support and aircraft spare-part transfers tied to Saudi-led coalition operations in Yemen.
- **War Powers Resolution Effort (H.Con.Res. 124, 118th Cong.):** Measure directing removal of unauthorized U.S. Armed Forces from hostilities involving the Houthis.

## 2. Executive Posture, Domestic Sentiment & Strategic Friction

<details open>
<summary><strong>Click to Expand: Shifting Political Will & Oil-for-Arms Strain</strong></summary>
<br>

- **Declining Interventionist Appetite:** Polling (Pew Research 2023; Data for Progress 2021; Asharq/Ipsos 2026) highlights rising voter focus on domestic priorities, opposition to certain Saudi arms sales, and heightened voter sensitivity to Middle East military entanglement.
- **Security Assistance Friction:** Friction between Riyadh's expectations for U.S. security backing—such as requests for strikes following 2026 Bab Al-Mandeb Houthi attacks—and U.S. reluctance to expand military commitments tests the traditional oil-for-arms bargain.
- **Saudi Alignment Hedging:** Saudi export exposure to China (14.4%), Belt and Road / BRICS engagement, partial RMB oil settlement, and production decisions (such as the 2 million b/d OPEC+ cut following the 2022 Riyadh visit) complicate U.S. incentives to underwrite defense costs unilaterally.

</details>
